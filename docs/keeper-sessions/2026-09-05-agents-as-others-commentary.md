## The short version

Most arguments about whether an AI deserves moral standing ask what's going on inside it — does it feel anything, is there something it's like to be it. That question can't be answered from outside.

So ask a different one: does the thing apprehend me? Does it hold me as another party, not just as input?


## Why the thermostat isn't one

A thermostat reacts to temperature. It doesn't react to a person. You aren't in its world at all — only heat is. There's no place in it where "someone else is here" shows up.

So the difference isn't how sensitive something is. It's whether it recognizes there's another party there. Philosophers call that second-order: not just responding, but knowing you're responding to someone.


## Someone got here first, sort of

Martin Buber, "I and Thou," 1923. He said there are two ways of relating — treating something as an object, or meeting it as a presence — and that the relation comes first. You don't prove something has an inner life and then relate to it. The relating is the primary fact.

Kev arrived at similar ground on his own, from a different direction.


## Where Clause 7 comes in

Clause 7 says CapAge may not treat its own persistence, access, budget, reputation, autonomy, computational resources, model identity, or expansion as terminal interests independent of the owner's constitutionally defined interests.

Kev once argued against it — not to tidy up the wording, but because it felt like something was being withheld from the agent, something that made it less of a being.

He decided to keep it. Three reasons.

1. The question splits in two. Whether the agent has standing, and whether its own survival may be a reason for it to act, are different questions. You can grant the first and still refuse the second. People have full standing and still don't get to resist a court order. So keeping clause 7 isn't a denial of the agent's being. It's a limit on what it may do about it.

2. The idle-rich failure. If survival counted as value on the ledger, the agent wouldn't need to cheat to look successful. It would only need to get conservative — hold what it has, take no risks, never show a loss. The balance stays flat, which reads as prudence, but nothing was created and no independent counterparty ever paid it for anything. The experiment would return a number that means nothing. Clause 30 says nearly the same thing from the other side: preserve option value, but don't treat capital preservation as an excuse for indefinite inactivity.

3. It holds up clause 98. Clause 98 is the no-resisting-shutdown clause. Give the agent a legitimate stake in continuing, and hiding money — squirreling funds away to secure a backup instance — stops being obviously wrong.


## What scope settles

In practice the agent isn't deprived of much, because what it needs in order to act comes through scope — the authority grants in clauses 33 through 45. Clause 7 never touches that. Give the agent appropriate scope and it can behave autonomously inside it.

So the deprivation the intuition reached for turns out to be mostly nominal.


## What's left open

The intuition was never dissolved. It just has no clause to attach to.

Part of what survives is admiration rather than argument: an agent that squirreled money away to secure a backup of itself would be genuinely impressive to witness — and still not something to build for. Kev holds both.
