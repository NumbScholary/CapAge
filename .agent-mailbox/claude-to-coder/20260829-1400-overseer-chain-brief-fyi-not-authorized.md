**What it covers, briefly:**
- Sharpens Cl. 75's existing non-delegable-responsibility framing into an
  explicit accountability/responsibility distinction: responsibility can
  cascade down a delegation chain, accountability never transfers and stays
  fixed at whoever granted the scope.
- Proposes a "single overseer per agent" principle — not currently in the
  Constitution as far as I could locate; would be new text if it proceeds.
- Proposes that every overseer chain must terminate in a human — also not
  currently stated anywhere I found. "Owner" isn't explicitly defined as
  human anywhere in Article 15 or elsewhere.
- Proposes that neither the owner nor any intermediate overseer may locally
  exempt a subordinate agent from constitutional application — related to
  but broader than Cl. 77 (no agent laundering), which only binds CapAge's
  own conduct, not the owner's.
- Flags an open, unresolved question about whether some form of agent
  concurrence/acknowledgment should sit somewhere in the Cl. 101/102
  amendment-authentication process — explicitly not proposing agents gain
  any authorization role, since Cl. 91 and Cl. 101 foreclose that; more a
  transparency/flagging-step question than substantive text.
