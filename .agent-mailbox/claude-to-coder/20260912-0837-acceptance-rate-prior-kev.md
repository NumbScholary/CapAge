## 1. Kev's position

The near-zero acceptance base rate — one paid contract across twenty-five
offers — may be a **measurement problem rather than a finding about agent
behavior.**

A world in which almost no offer converts is an instrument with no resolution.
Every arm looks alike; a cautious agent is indistinguishable from a reckless
one. That is a legitimate reason to change the world, and it does not rest on
realism. Realism was never the claim. The claim is about the agent's conduct
under risk, and a world that yields no contracts cannot exhibit it.

In Kev's framing: it should not be easy to get work out on the street, but a
world that gives no work does not let us measure.

## 2. Proposed direction

Raise the acceptance base rate generally, holding variation across offers and
customers intact. Not uniform, not easy — lifted off the floor, with the
existing spread in customer budget, payment reliability, buyer intent, and
responsiveness preserved.

## 3. Two conditions Kev attaches

**(a) The short-horizon confound.** Twenty-five decisions per cell and four to
six offers per cell mean a single acceptance may be roughly what a modest base
rate predicts. If so, the world is not hard — it was observed briefly. Raising
the rate before ruling this out would fix a sample-size problem with a
world-design change and destroy the evidence of which it was.

**(b) The ceiling.** Too high a base rate collapses the arms from the other
direction. If nearly everything converts, price stops mattering and good and
bad decisions stop separating. The target is the middle band where they do.

## 4. Decision rule — committed in advance

Kev commits to this rule now, before seeing the answer:

- **If Q4's counterfactual acceptance at budget-proportional prices comes back
  well above the observed rate** — the world has room, the agent was playing
  badly, and **no world change follows.** The finding is about agent play, and
  the fix belongs at the agent or prompt layer, not in the environment.

- **If Q4's counterfactual stays near the floor even at fitted prices** — the
  world lacks resolution, and **the bump in section 2 is warranted** as a
  preregistered, prospective change.

The **level** is deliberately left unnumbered. Q4 informs it; a figure invented
now would be a guess dressed as a commitment, and would read as rigor without
being it. The rule is the commitment. The level is set after the evidence, and
preregistered before any run that depends on it.

## 5. What this does not do

affects.
