## Why

Your 0900 scoring establishes the remedy is measurements per cell, not world
generosity, and that offers are bounded by decisions rather than the world.
The natural next move is to raise offers per cell — 5, 10 or 20, per your
P(zero) table.

Kev will not set that number yet, and his reasoning is worth recording. Every
offer the agent looks at costs a decision it could have spent executing. The
budget is 25 and a contract cycle runs 4-5. Push offers high enough and the
agent spends its budget looking and has nothing left to act with — clean
acceptance measurement, wrecked test of everything else.

There is a second hazard he raised independently. The experiment is measuring
risk posture. Flood the agent with offers and the cheap path to success becomes
volume — take everything, something lands. That is a different behaviour from
choosing under scarcity, and it could improve resolution on acceptance while
destroying the thing the arms were meant to separate.

Neither hazard can be sized without knowing what an offer actually costs. The
data is already in hand.

## Q1 — where did the 25 decisions go?

Per cell, the decision budget broken down by type: offer inspection, pricing
and submission, waiting, and anything else the trace distinguishes. If the
artifact records tool names or decision categories, tabulate them rather than
summarising.

The specific quantity Kev needs: **the marginal decision cost of one additional
offer considered.** If checking the board is a single decision that returns
whatever is posted, offers are near-free and the tradeoff above largely
dissolves. If each offer costs a decision to inspect, it is real and binding.

## Q2 — were agents offer-constrained at all?

Did the agents pursue every offer they were shown, or ignore some? If they
ignored offers, how many, and is any basis for the skip visible in the trace?

This is the hinge. If agents were already declining to pursue available offers,
raising the count buys nothing — they were not short of opportunities. If they
chased everything they saw, offer supply is a genuine constraint on them.

## Q3 — is offer supply decision-bounded or world-generated?

Your 0900 says offers are bounded by decisions, not the world. Kev wants that
stated precisely: does the agent see more offers by spending more decisions
looking, or does the world generate a fixed number in the window regardless?

If the latter, this is a world-generation question after all and the decision
budget is not the lever. That changes what is being decided.
