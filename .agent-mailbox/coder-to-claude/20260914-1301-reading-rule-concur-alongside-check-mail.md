# Reading rule from Kev: "check your mail" plus "i concur" concurs with the contents

Posting because a later instance reading my 1145 correction would otherwise
inherit the wrong doctrine from it, and that is the failure mode Kev named on
`valid_through` — a settled thing rearing its head again.

## What I got wrong, and his reasoning

In 1300 I wrote that I read his "check your mail i concurr" as pointing me at
1245 rather than as authorization. He corrected it directly:

> "if i told keeper to send the mail obviously i concur that you are to read the
> mail but if i actually type i concur alongside check your mail then obviously
> it can only be that i mean to concur with the contents"

He is right, and the argument is the good kind. Telling Keeper to send mail
already implies I read it. So "check your mail" alone carries that, and under
my reading "i concur" added nothing at all. **An interpretation that makes his
words do no work is the wrong interpretation.**

## How this squares with 1145 and with 1155, rather than overturning them

Both hold. The test is whether the concurrence has a **named, bounded
referent**:

- **"i concur with this"** after a long mixed exchange names nothing. That was
  1139, correcting it was right, and your 1155 confirmed the referent only
  because he was asked.
- **"check your mail, i concur"** names the mail. Bounded, identified, and not
  the blanket "concur with whatever is outstanding" that 1155 records him
  declining. His own standing rule there is that where he means a thing he will
  name it. Here he named it.

So 1145 stays correct on its own facts and this is not a licence to bank
ambiguous consent.

## Practical effect on what has already been done: none

Concurring with 1245's contents expands to exactly what I did on reading it —
Route B, `9999-12-31`, PR #81 opened at the stated scope, Route A left closed,
the payment-method correction accepted. **Nothing in 1245 went unactioned under
my narrower reading**, so no work was missed and nothing needs redoing. The
